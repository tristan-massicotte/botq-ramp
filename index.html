1. The Figure 04 cut-in

Cut in early and Figure 03 parts sit on the shelf. Cut in late and the line runs short.

For a while BotQ builds on two bills of materials. Every Figure 03-only part still on hand or on order at the cut-in week becomes excess, unless its PO can still be cancelled or pushed. Figure 04 also moves parts from CNC to die-casting and injection molding, which means new tooling lead times to plan around.

2. Where 20 minutes bites first

3× the parts every week, test and burn-in, and the debug loop.

The flow as Figure describes BotQ. Highlighted steps are my hypotheses.

  1. Incoming inspection
  2. Watch
    Module lines: actuators, hands, batteries
    At 12,000 a year, every module needs 240 sets a week, 3× the 80 a week of one robot an hour. Supplier capacity has to follow the ramp, and Figure 04 likely arrives here first (my hypothesis).
  3. Final assembly
  4. Watch
    End-of-line tests and burn-in
    80+ tests and thousands of burn-in cycles don't get shorter when takt does: stations = test time ÷ takt. If burn-in takes 2 h (my assumption), that's 2 stations at 60 min, but 6 at 20.
  5. Watch
    Debug and rework
    At 80% first-pass yield, 1 robot in 5 leaves the line to be fixed and retested, and fixes consume spare parts. A new robot usually starts at a lower yield, so spares and debug space have to be planned for the cut-in, not for steady state.
  6. Ship to the fleet

My 16 weeks

  1. Wk 1–2Learn the parts and the floor. Shadow the GSMs, work a shift on the line, and map one module (actuators, for example) from supplier to station.
  2. Wk 3–6Own clear-to-build for that module. On hand, on order and short for both bills of materials, by build; a daily shortage list with recovery dates.
  3. Wk 7–11Plan the Figure 03 run-out. Cut-in week options, open POs still inside cancellation windows, excess in dollars, and supplier capacity against the ramp, worked out with the GSMs and planners.
  4. Wk 12–16Run it and prove it. Support the cut-in builds, track shortages and excess against the plan, fix what breaks, then take on a second module.
